#! nostage
#
# Jafna: a flattening swing raises low ground for stone, and nothing else costs any.
#
# Eight claims:
#
#   1. Digging is free. A swing that pulls a mound down further than the hoe's one metre takes no
#      stone.
#   2. A swing with nothing to raise costs nothing and puts nothing in the middle of the screen.
#   3. With enough stone one swing lifts its whole circle to the height it aims at, and only its
#      circle: ground halfway to the edge is on the height, ground just outside the circle is
#      where it was, and there is a step between them.
#   4. With no stone the ground comes up the hoe's own metre and stops there, about a metre short,
#      and the middle of the screen says "Not enough Stone".
#   5. With too little stone the swing lifts the middle of the circle and bills only that: both
#      stones leave the pack, the middle comes up to the height, the middle of the screen says
#      "Not enough Stone", and nothing is left owing for the next swing.
#   6. The stone pays for each point's whole way up past its free metre, not only for the part the
#      hoe's own swing would have been cut short on: at the hoe's own reach two stones pay for no
#      more than the middle of a swing, and both leave the pack.
#   7. Raising with stone needs a workbench in range, as Raise ground does. Out of its range a
#      swing that would be paid takes no stone, the ground comes up the hoe's own free metre and
#      no further, and both the build panel and the middle of the screen say a Workbench is needed.
#   8. Only your own swing is reshaped. Devkit's `flatten` is a terrain op that is not a swing, and
#      it levels to its own height even with its circle on a flat at another height, where a build
#      that reshaped every op continues that flat instead.
#
# NO STAGE. The `#! nostage` line at the top turns off the ground Devkit makes ready before the
# first step (from Devkit 96df32a). That stage levels a disc 30 m round you with a 4 m ramp beyond
# it and flags every point of it as shaped, and a shaped point is exactly what Jafna's continued
# flat reads. So on staged ground a swing here can find the staged height under it and continue
# that instead of aiming at the mound top, and the fills this file is about never run. It needs the
# untouched ground NEEDS asks for, not a staged one.
#
# HOW A SWING AIMS ABOVE THE GROUND. A scenario cannot hold Left Alt and Jafna has no console
# command for a held height. It does not need either. The hoe's Level ground does not aim at the
# height under the crosshair: Player.UpdatePlacementGhost puts the ghost where the crosshair is but
# at GetGroundHeight of your own position, and only vanilla's AltPlace turns that off. AltPlace is
# Left Shift by default (ZInput binds it there, and so does this machine's saved binding), so it
# is a different key from Jafna's Left Alt, and Left Alt pins that same height, your own. So a
# swing taken standing on a mound two metres high aims two metres above the ground in front of it,
# and a swing taken from the ground at a mound aims below the mound. `build` presses the place
# button through the game's own path, so these are real swings with Jafna's patches and the stone
# in them, and `mark` reads the same ground height the ghost does.
#
# The continued flat cannot do this job. It is taken from the shaped points nearest the swing, and
# Raise ground and every hoe swing leave shaped points at the old height round their edge, so no
# swing at untouched low ground finds a flat above it.
#
# THE MOUNDS. Two Raise ground on one spot. raise_v2 was read out of the game files: radius 2, lift
# 1, power 0.1, Stone x2. That leaves a block about three metres square and 1.8 to 2 metres high
# that drops to the old ground within a metre. Mound B is 3 m ahead of where you start, mound A
# is 8 m behind you, mound C 20 m behind you, and mound D 10 m ahead and 9 m to your left.
# Sections 4 and 3 swing from either side of mound A, sections 5 and 6 from either side of mound
# C, and section 7 from mound D. Swings from a mound are taken standing half a metre off its
# middle toward the spot, because from the middle, on some headings, the mound's own corner hides
# a spot three metres out.
#
# Each mound is measured against the ground it is built on, not the ground you build it from: the
# run steps onto the spot, marks it there, and steps three metres back to build. The second run in
# a game, on 2026-09-28, marked where you stood instead, and mound C read +2.83 against a wanted
# 1.9. The mound was fine. Its two Raise ground put 0.94 and 0.96 m on the spot, as they put 0.94
# and 0.94 on mound B and 0.97 and 0.96 on mound A, read off the `build` lines, whose "up of the
# spot" is the ghost's height less the ground at the spot in the frame the button was pressed.
# The spot's own ground was simply 0.93 m above where you stood, three metres away (mound B's was
# 0.09 below, mound A's 0.12 below). Jafna was not in it: a raise op is handed straight back to
# vanilla by every one of its terrain patches, and it logged nothing between section 4's swing and
# that check. That ground fell 2.7 m over the 23 m from where you start to mound C, about 12
# percent against the 1 in 20 NEEDS asks for, and every other check passed on it. So the mark is
# now on the spot, and the check says whether two Raise ground landed and nothing about the slope.
#
# The same goes for every height here that reads ground away from a mound. Section 7's spot, section
# 4's spot 2.5 m past its swing and section 3's point outside its circle are each marked standing on
# them before the swing, and the check reads how far they moved. The last two were read against the
# mound top until the fourth run in a game, on 2026-09-29, on the guess that the ground round a
# mound lies 1.9 m below its top, and that run had ground falling about 12 in 100 across mound A.
# Before each of those swings a gate reads the ground under you, which is the height the swing aims
# at, against the marked point, and refuses when it is too close for the check to tell a right
# swing from a wrong one, so a slope too steep for a check says so there instead of reading as a
# Jafna fault. The heights still read against the ground under you (section 5's middle, section 3's
# middle and halfway point) are on ground the swing fills to that very height, so no slope moves
# them.
#
# The step back is `landed`, not `wait 2`. It comes straight after the step onto the spot, with only
# the mark between, and the game's two seconds are counted in physics steps of 0.02 s from the one
# that ended the first move. A hundred of those add up to a hair under 2 in single precision, which
# TeleportTo refuses, and `wait 2` reaches the hundred and first only by the slack of a frame or
# two: at 60 frames a second it is refused about one time in fifty, and at 100 about one in two. A
# refused step back leaves you on the spot, so the mound goes up three metres further on and every
# section after it swings at the wrong ground. `landed` reads the cooldown itself.
#
# THE WORKBENCH. Raise ground is a workbench piece: raise_v2's Piece names the workbench as its
# m_craftingStation (read out of its asset bundle on 2026-09-28, the same station woodwall names),
# and Player.HaveRequirements refuses a piece whose station is not within build range before it
# looks at the stone. The first run in a game had four Stone and no bench, and every Raise ground
# was refused with "Missing requirement", so no mound was ever built and every check after one
# read ground nothing had raised.
#
# Since 2026-09-28 a fill needs the same bench in range of where you stand, so the bench has to
# reach every swing that pays or runs short as well as every mound. It stands 11 m behind you and
# 10 m to your left. Its range is 20 m (m_rangeBuild off a Devkit rip of piece_workbench, with no
# extensions), measured flat from the bench to you. That covers the four places a mound is built
# from (15, 10, 16 and 18 m) and every place sections 1 to 6 swing from, the farthest being
# section 4's first swing on mound B at 17.6 m, and leaves out section 7's swing on mound D at
# 21.5 m. Until section 7 the bench stood 14 m behind you, which put that swing on mound B 20.2 m
# from it: just out of range, so section 4 would have got the workbench message and not "Not
# enough Stone". It is clear of every swing, every height read and every camera ray here.
#
# THE SELECTED ENTRY. `build` leaves the hoe on the entry it built until the run ends, so after a
# mound the hoe is still on Raise ground, which Jafna's panel says nothing about. Anything that
# reads the panel after a mound selects Level ground again first.
#
# THE HELD HEIGHT. The first run had one switched on part-way through, by Alt+Tab: Left Alt is the
# hold key, and the build that ran acted the moment it went down, which switching windows does
# while the game still has the focus. Every swing after it went to the held height instead of your
# own, and nothing said so. That is fixed since: the key counts when it is let go, and not when
# another key was pressed while it was down or the window lost focus in between. No step here can
# press a key or take the focus away, so manual steps 9 to 12 are how to see it. The check stays
# anyway: before each section's swing the panel is read for HOLDING, and if that check fails, the
# run was disturbed and nothing after it in that section means anything.
#
# THE PRICE. A point is charged for the part of its way up that is past its free metre. Every point
# here starts with its whole metre, and the low ground round a mound is about 1.9 m below its top,
# so each low point a paid swing lifts costs about 0.9 cubic metres. One stone buys 4.1 (Raise
# ground's Stone x2 over the 8.27 cubic metres one Raise ground adds to flat ground).
#
# THE ORDER, AND THE CREDIT. Jafna takes stone whole and keeps the rest until you log out, so a
# run can start with up to a stone already paid for. A correct build passes either way, but a fresh
# session is still the better run, because a leftover can quietly pay for a small charge a broken
# build makes where it should not. A build that charges for digging is caught in 99 fresh runs in
# 100, and in about 1 in 3 when a leftover is carried in (see the broken builds below).
#
# Sections 4, 2 and 1 come first because they expect no stone to move. The first swing is one no
# stone can pay for: from mound B, with an empty pack and Crafting at 100 so it covers 12 metres,
# its flat top wants about 70 to 115 cubic metres. It runs short, and that is claim 4's message.
# Claims 2 and 1 follow, then claim 4's height, then claims 5, 6, 3, 7 and 8.
#
# Claims 5 and 6 each give two stones to a swing they cannot pay for in full, and check that both
# are gone. A short swing stops at a whole ring of points, so it can leave most of a stone paid
# for. The rings round the middle hold four points each, 3.6 cubic metres, less than a stone, so
# what a correct build leaves unspent there is under a stone and it takes both, whatever it carried
# in. That leftover is why claim 3 does not check how much stone its swing takes. Claim 3's swing
# is also the proof that claims 5 and 6 left no debt: it needs about 4 to 7 Stone and is given 20,
# so a correct build is never short there.
#
# Section 7 comes last of the swings. On a correct build it takes no stone at all, so nothing the
# others left paid for can move its count. It needs a mound of its own at the edge of the bench's
# range, built from just inside it and swung from just outside. Section 8 swings nothing and reads
# no stone. It comes after all of them because it digs into section 3's flat top, which it needs as
# the flat at another height, and nothing after it reads that ground again.
#
# Claim 4's height and claim 3 are on opposite sides of mound A on purpose. A swing that runs out
# leaves ground a metre up and flat, and the next swing on that spot continues that flat instead of
# your height, so stone given afterwards raises nothing there.
#

#
